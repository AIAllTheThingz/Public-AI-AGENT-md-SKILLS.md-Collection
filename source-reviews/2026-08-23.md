## Material findings

### Product Management (`disciplines/product-management`)

- ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 is an appropriate authoritative starting point for requirements-engineering concepts. The package uses repository-authored requirements for identifiers, acceptance criteria, sources, ownership, change history, and traceability; it does not claim a clause-level implementation of the standard.
- ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288 is an appropriate authoritative starting point for system life-cycle framing. The package's product decisions and evidence states remain repository controls rather than assertions that the source defines this repository's exact workflow.
- ISO/IEC 25010 is an appropriate authoritative starting point for considering product quality characteristics. The package requires applicable nonfunctional requirements without reproducing the quality model or implying evaluation, certification, or completeness.
- The final revision separates acceptance decisions from execution and evidence states. Each criterion records `Pass`, `Fail`, `Blocked`, `NotRun`, or justified `NotApplicable` for the exact reviewed artifact and conditions; `Tested`, `Reviewed`, implementation presence, or output presence does not imply acceptance. These are repository-authored fail-closed evidence controls and are not attributed as exact source requirements.
- No source-driven correction to the package's registered URLs, high-level source descriptions, applicability boundary, or limitations was required by this review.

### User Experience (`disciplines/user-experience`)

- ISO 9241-210 is an appropriate authoritative starting point for human-centred design, and ISO 9241-11 is an appropriate starting point for usability definitions and contextual usability concepts. The package does not reproduce either standard or claim conformance.
- WCAG 2.2 remains a W3C Recommendation and is correctly treated as an accessibility source rather than proof that UX research or UX validation occurred.
- The WAI-ARIA Authoring Practices Guide remains relevant public implementation guidance for accessible patterns. It does not replace native semantics, WCAG evaluation, assistive-technology testing, or the repository's separate Accessibility discipline.
- The final revision keeps research state, UX-validation state, and accessibility evidence independent. It defines `Performed` as the positive research state only when actual research was completed and attributable evidence exists, retains `NotRun`, `Blocked`, and justified `NotApplicable` for accurate non-run outcomes, and exposes that complete research-state set in the evidence template. These states are repository-authored evidence controls; no source-driven correction to the independence boundary or registered URLs was required by this review.

### Site Reliability Engineering (`disciplines/sre`)

- The Google SRE book and workbook remain appropriate public starting points for SLOs, monitoring, overload and cascading-failure behavior, production launch review, practical reliability implementation, incident response, and controlled release practices.
- Current Kubernetes resource-management guidance distinguishes requests and limits for resources such as CPU, memory, and ephemeral storage. Current Horizontal Pod Autoscaling guidance describes metrics-based replica decisions, stabilization, and scaling policies. These sources support contextual resource and autoscaling decisions only when Kubernetes applies.
- Current AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud reliability guidance remains appropriate for the selected provider context. Provider guidance does not create a portable proof that another platform or a specific deployment is reliable.
- The final revision's production-readiness contract was re-reviewed across the standard, package instructions and README, evidence and review templates, adoption checklist, and example. Privacy is a distinct readiness area when applicable so Security evidence cannot silently stand in for privacy review. Data migration is also a distinct area when applicable and requires representative rehearsal, recovery planning, irreversible-change handling, accepted residual risk, explicit go/no-go criteria, decision authority, and rollback or roll-forward behavior.
- The package's per-area readiness results, separate overall result, scaling applicability states, decision authority, Privacy boundary, and Data migration controls are repository-authored evidence controls. The final revision permits an overall scaling-strategy `Verified` claim only when every applicable area is `Verified` with representative evidence and a supported envelope; `Applicable`, `NotRun`, or `Blocked` areas keep that overall claim unresolved, while `NotApplicable` requires justification. This review does not attribute those exact controls to the reliability sources or treat backup, restore, deployment, or source review as a readiness or scaling pass.
- No registered source URL or high-level source description required correction.

### Testing and Quality Engineering (`disciplines/testing`)

- The Google SRE sources support treating performance, capacity, failure, and recovery evidence as contextual operational evidence rather than a universal benchmark.
- Kubernetes resource and autoscaling documentation supports testing exact resource requests, limits, observed metrics, stabilization, and scaling behavior when Kubernetes is the system under test.
- The package's baseline, load, stress, spike, soak or endurance, scaling, failure-under-load, and recovery-under-load taxonomy and its `Applicable`, `NotApplicable`, `NotRun`, `Blocked`, and `Tested` states are repository-authored controls. This review does not claim those exact categories are mandated by the sources.
- The final revision's Testing surface was re-reviewed across the numbered package rule, standard, package README, adoption example, adoption and review checklists, and evidence template after the performance matrix added independent per-test owner, explicit execution authorization and safeguards, safe stop conditions, execution state, and separate `Pass`, `Fail`, `Blocked`, or justified `NotApplicable` outcome. `Tested` does not imply `Pass`; a performance or scalability validated claim requires every applicable type to be `Tested` with a current explicit `Pass` against authorized criteria for the exact artifact and representative conditions. Any applicable failed, blocked, not-run, missing, stale, or different-artifact result prevents that claim. These accountability, safety, and outcome fields are repository-authored evidence controls; the review found them consistent with the cited sources' contextual, controlled performance and failure-testing boundaries and does not attribute the exact matrix fields to those sources.
- No load, resilience, recovery, scalability, or production test was run as part of source review, and no supported operating envelope was established.

### Product Inception Lifecycle (`governance/PRODUCT_INCEPTION_LIFECYCLE.md`)

- ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148 and ISO/IEC/IEEE 15288 support the document's high-level requirements and life-cycle framing as authoritative starting points.
- The final revision's explicit selection boundary was re-reviewed: the lifecycle applies only when explicitly selected for a project or change, and repository, governance, profile, or Product Management package adoption does not select it. Once selected, its controls govern the selected scope.
- The final revision gives the Design Gate an explicit gate-level decision of `Pass`, `Fail`, or `Blocked` and requires an explicit Build Gate `Pass` before normal production implementation starts, while retaining the separately authorized prototype or experiment exception and its prohibition on silent transition to production implementation.
- The final revision assigns the complete normative lifecycle contract stable rule IDs `GOV-PRODUCT-INCEPTION-001` through `GOV-PRODUCT-INCEPTION-012`. The identifiers cover explicit selection, evidence-state integrity, the Concept, Requirements, Design, and Build gates, the prototype boundary, lifecycle transitions, the production-candidate gate, traceability and re-review, exceptions, and completion claims; `GOV-PRODUCT-INCEPTION-006` identifies the Build Gate requirement for evidence mapping and exact-rule exceptions.
- The final revision uses a closed set of lifecycle evidence states and keeps gate decisions separate from evidence states. It requires the Concept, Requirements, and Design Gates to be `Pass`, explicit Product Management package selection, complete requirement traceability, and selection or justified omission of every other applicable package before the Build Gate can pass.
- The final revision requires every material requirement to be implemented and every applicable functional and nonfunctional acceptance criterion to have a current explicit `Pass` for the exact candidate, supported configuration, and representative environment before `production-candidate`. `Tested`, `Reviewed`, implementation presence, and test-output presence do not imply `Pass`; any failed, blocked, not-run, missing, stale, or different-candidate acceptance result prevents the transition, and readiness evidence does not replace acceptance evidence.
- The final revision requires explicit Site Reliability Engineering package selection and its complete Production Readiness Standard before `production-candidate`. The exact candidate must retain prerequisite gate passes, an overall readiness `Pass`, and a complete matrix covering Deployment, Rollback and recovery, Backup and restore, Data migration, Observability, Capacity, Security, Privacy, Failure behavior, Operations, Cost, and Limitations and risks; every applicable area must pass and no applicable area may remain `Fail`, `Blocked`, or `NotRun`.
- The final revision permits `production` only while the complete `production-candidate` boundary remains satisfied and accountable delegated approval is recorded. It permits `scaled-production` only while the `production` boundary remains satisfied, the SRE package is selected, the complete Scaling Strategy Standard has an overall `Verified` decision, and every applicable scaling area is `Verified` with representative evidence and a supported envelope. Any `Applicable`, `NotRun`, or `Blocked` area prevents that state, and every `NotApplicable` area requires justification.
- The final revision states that a `beta` label does not authorize production exposure and that exceptions cannot convert failed, blocked, not-run, or missing-package evidence into a passing gate, verified claim, or later lifecycle state.
- The applicability boundary; named Concept, Requirements, Design, and Build gates; stable rule IDs; gate decisions; authorization boundary; and prototype exception are repository-authored governance controls. This review does not attribute those exact controls to either ISO source. No source-driven correction to the lifecycle's registered URLs or high-level requirements and life-cycle framing was required.
